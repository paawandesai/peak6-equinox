Glossary

Equinox compares Kalshi and Polymarket contracts, decides how each pair is related, and simulates where an order would fill best. Each entry starts with the plain words the pages use, then the technical name in brackets. Prices are per $1 payout, so they lie between 0 and 1.

How two contracts can relate

Two contracts are the same bet only if buying YES on both pays out identically in every possible outcome. Types are listed strongest first. Only routable types are ever used to route an order.

Words on the decision and pair pages

Safety charge (basis-risk penalty)
A small charge per contract for the chance that two matched contracts settle differently. Equinox adds it to a partner venue's cost before comparing, so a substitute only wins when it is cheaper even after the charge. It is the safety risk score times a set amount (one cent per contract by default).
Safety risk (basis-risk score)
How much two "same bet" contracts could still settle differently, for example because of edge-case rules. Scored from 0 to 1: "low" below 0.20, "moderate" 0.20 to 0.49, "high" 0.50 or more. "Not scored" means no score was computed.
Confidence (relationship confidence)
How sure the matcher is that a pair has the stated type, from 0 to 1. "High" is 0.90 or more, which is also the minimum to route; "medium" is 0.70 to 0.89; "low" is below 0.70. On a decision page this reads "we're not sure enough these are the same bet" when a pair is below 0.90.
Total per contract (all-in cost per $1 payout)
What one contract costs per $1 payout once everything is counted: the price plus the venue fee plus the safety charge. The router picks the lowest total; ties go to the deeper book. Pages show it in cents ("0.80¢") and the order total in dollars ("$0.80 for 100 contracts").
Price (VWAP, volume-weighted average price)
The average price paid when an order fills by consuming the order book from the best price to worse ones, weighted by size.
Fee (venue trading fee)
What the venue charges per contract, from its published fee schedule.
Too old to trust (stale)
The price was fetched too long before the decision, so the router does not use it. The page says how many minutes old it was.
Taken too far apart (skew)
Two prices were fetched too many seconds apart to compare fairly, so the older one is set aside.
Nobody is selling / Only N on offer (insufficient_depth, no book)
The order book has no offers at any price ("nobody is selling"), or fewer contracts than the order needs ("only N contracts on offer").
Guaranteed loss (sure_loss)
Buying would cost more than the contract can ever pay out (more than $1 per $1 payout), so the router never picks it.
Technical details (the audit layer)
The collapsed section at the bottom of a decision or pair page. It keeps the original codes, instrument IDs, configuration values, the router's own explanation and a link to the raw JSON.
Near miss (a related pair that came close)
A related pair that came close: the same subject with one term off, or a pair an external fact was consulted for but did not close. Near misses are listed on Near misses with the rule that separated them.
Closed by a fact (fact closed)
A term difference that a documented external fact explains, such as the current Fed upper bound, which lets a "rate rises 25 bps" contract be compared with a "rate above X%" contract. The pair becomes equivalent_given_facts. A fact never overrides a veto on subject, deadline or source.
Round (fetch round)
One fetch of both venues' contracts and order books, taken at one moment. Every page reads from one round, named with its "as of" time.
Pinned (pinned round)
The operator has fixed the round the public pages show, for the demo. While a round is pinned, no page calls the venues.
rate bps (Fed moves)
Basis points of an interest rate: 1 bp is 0.01 percentage point. "+25 bps" means the Fed raises its target range by a quarter point, for example from 3.75-4.00 % to 4.00-4.25 %. Used in Fed contract titles.
price bps (of a $1 payout)
Basis points of a contract price: 1 bp is 0.0001 of the $1 payout, which is 0.01 ¢ per $1 payout. 100 bps is 1 ¢. Used for slippage and cost gaps in the technical details.
Slippage (VWAP versus best quote)
How much worse the price paid is than the best quoted price, in bps, because a large order eats into deeper, more expensive levels of the book.

Veto codes (technical details)

A veto is a hard rule that stops a pair from being called the same bet. A *_mismatch code means both contracts state the term and they differ. A *_unknown code means at least one contract's term could not be read. Equinox never guesses: an unknown term is a veto too.
